Foundational Concepts

Basic Chemistry

▼

The chemistry of atoms and bonds is the language of biology.

  • Atoms, elements, and the periodic table
  • Ionic, covalent, and hydrogen bonds
  • Acids, bases, and the pH scale (0-14)
  • Buffers and their role in maintaining pH
  • Chemical reactions: reactants, products, energy
  • Polarity and electronegativity
Khan Academy: Chemistry of Life →
💧

Properties of Water

▼

Water's unique properties make life on Earth possible - Unit 1 depends on this.

  • Polarity: partial positive charge on H, partial negative on O
  • Hydrogen bonding between water molecules
  • Cohesion (water-water) and adhesion (water-other surfaces)
  • High specific heat: water resists temperature change
  • High heat of vaporisation: evaporation cools organisms
  • Universal solvent: dissolves polar and ionic substances
  • Ice is less dense than liquid water - why ice floats
Khan Academy: Water and Life →
🧬

Biological Macromolecules

▼

Four classes of macromolecules are the molecular building blocks of all life.

  • Carbohydrates: glucose, starch, cellulose, glycogen
  • Lipids: fats (energy storage), phospholipids (membranes), steroids
  • Proteins: amino acids joined by peptide bonds; 4 levels of structure
  • Nucleic acids: DNA (deoxyribose, A-T/C-G) and RNA (ribose, A-U/C-G)
  • Dehydration synthesis builds polymers; hydrolysis breaks them down
  • Structure determines function - a core AP Biology theme
Khan Academy: Macromolecules →
🔬

Cell Biology Basics

▼

Cells are the basic unit of life. Know organelle structure and function thoroughly.

  • Prokaryotic (no nucleus) vs eukaryotic (has nucleus and organelles)
  • Key organelles: nucleus, ribosome, rough/smooth ER, Golgi, mitochondria, lysosome, chloroplast
  • Cell membrane: fluid mosaic model - phospholipid bilayer + proteins + cholesterol
  • Diffusion: passive movement from high to low concentration
  • Osmosis: water moves across a membrane toward higher solute concentration
  • Tonicity: hypertonic (cell shrinks), hypotonic (cell swells), isotonic (no change)
  • Surface area to volume ratio limits cell size
Khan Academy: Cell Structure →
⚡

Energy and Metabolism

▼

All living things require energy - understanding how cells capture and use it is critical.

  • ATP: adenosine triphosphate - the cell's energy currency
  • Enzymes lower activation energy; specific to their substrate
  • Enzyme activity affected by temperature, pH, substrate concentration, inhibitors
  • Photosynthesis: CO2 + H2O + light energy → glucose + O2 (in chloroplasts)
  • Cellular respiration: glucose + O2 → CO2 + H2O + ATP (in mitochondria)
  • Fermentation: anaerobic ATP production - produces lactic acid or ethanol
  • Chemiosmosis: proton gradient drives ATP synthesis via ATP synthase
Khan Academy: Cellular Energetics →
🧫

Genetics Fundamentals

▼

Heredity and molecular genetics are central themes across multiple AP Biology units.

  • DNA structure: antiparallel double helix; A-T and C-G base pairing
  • DNA replication: semiconservative; occurs during S phase
  • Central dogma: DNA → (transcription) → mRNA → (translation) → protein
  • Mitosis: 2 genetically identical diploid daughter cells
  • Meiosis: 4 haploid gametes; crossing over increases diversity
  • Mendelian inheritance: dominant/recessive, Punnett squares
  • Non-Mendelian: incomplete dominance, codominance, sex-linkage
Khan Academy: Genetics →
🦋

Evolution and Natural Selection

▼

Evolution is the unifying principle of all biology. Unit 7 is 13-20% of the exam.

  • Darwin's four conditions: variation, heritability, overproduction, differential survival
  • Natural selection acts on phenotype; evolution changes allele frequencies
  • Types of selection: directional, stabilising, disruptive
  • Genetic drift: random allele frequency changes - most significant in small populations
  • Hardy-Weinberg: p squared + 2pq + q squared = 1; p + q = 1
  • Evidence: fossil record, comparative anatomy, biogeography, molecular data
  • Speciation: new species arise through reproductive isolation
Khan Academy: Natural Selection →
🌍

Ecology Overview

▼

Life is organised at multiple scales. Ecology connects biology to the real world.

  • Hierarchy: individual → population → community → ecosystem → biosphere
  • Abiotic (non-living) vs biotic (living) factors
  • Food chains and webs; 10% rule for energy transfer between trophic levels
  • Population growth: exponential (J-curve) vs logistic (S-curve, carrying capacity K)
  • Ecological relationships: predation, competition, mutualism, parasitism
  • Nutrient cycles: carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus
  • Biodiversity and ecosystem stability
Khan Academy: Ecology →

Course Preview: The 8 Units

Here is what you will study throughout the year. Percentages show how much each unit is weighted on the AP Exam.

Unit 1 · 8-11% of exam

Chemistry of Life

~9-11 class periods

Sets the chemical foundation - water properties, macromolecule structure and function, and the reactions that build and break them.

  • Water
  • Elements of Life
  • Macromolecules
  • Carbohydrates
  • Lipids
  • Nucleic Acids
  • Proteins
Unit 2 · 10-13% of exam

Cells

~14-16 class periods

The cell as the basic unit of life - organelle function, membrane structure, surface area to volume, and all forms of transport.

  • Cell Structure
  • Cell Size
  • Plasma Membrane
  • Membrane Transport
  • Tonicity
  • Compartmentalisation
  • Endosymbiosis
Unit 3 · 12-16% of exam

Cellular Energetics

~12-14 class periods

How cells capture, store, and use energy. Enzymes, photosynthesis, cellular respiration, and fermentation.

  • Enzymes
  • Environmental impacts
  • Cellular energy
  • Photosynthesis
  • Cellular Respiration
  • Fermentation
Unit 4 · 10-15% of exam

Cell Communication and Cell Cycle

~12-14 class periods

How cells send and receive signals via transduction pathways, and how the cell cycle controls growth and division.

  • Cell Communication
  • Signal Transduction
  • Feedback
  • Cell Cycle
  • Checkpoints
  • Apoptosis
Unit 5 · 8-11% of exam

Heredity

~8-10 class periods

How traits are passed from parent to offspring through meiosis, Mendelian genetics, and non-Mendelian patterns.

  • Meiosis
  • Genetic Diversity
  • Mendelian Genetics
  • Non-Mendelian Genetics
  • Environmental Effects
Unit 6 · 12-16% of exam

Gene Expression and Regulation

~18-20 class periods

The molecular mechanisms of gene expression - DNA replication, transcription, translation, gene regulation, mutations, and biotechnology.

  • DNA and RNA
  • DNA Replication
  • Transcription
  • Translation
  • Gene Regulation
  • Mutations
  • Biotechnology
Unit 7 · 13-20% of exam

Natural Selection

~19-21 class periods

The highest-weighted unit - natural selection, population genetics, Hardy-Weinberg, evidence for evolution, phylogeny, and speciation.

  • Natural Selection
  • Population Genetics
  • Hardy-Weinberg
  • Evidence for Evolution
  • Phylogeny
  • Speciation
Unit 8 · 10-15% of exam

Ecology

~19-21 class periods

How organisms interact with each other and their environments - from individual responses to ecosystem-level disruptions.

  • Energy Flow
  • Population Ecology
  • Density Effects
  • Community Ecology
  • Biodiversity
  • Ecosystem Disruptions

The 6 Science Practices

AP Biology tests scientific thinking skills, not just content recall. These six practices are assessed throughout every unit and explicitly tested on the AP Exam.

Science Practice 1

Concept Explanation

The ability to accurately describe and explain biological concepts, processes, and principles using precise scientific language.

  • Describe biological concepts and processes
  • Explain biological concepts and processes
  • Explain a piece of data, observation, or pattern
Science Practice 2

Visual Representations

Using and interpreting visual tools such as diagrams, graphs, and models to understand biological relationships.

  • Describe characteristics of visual representations
  • Explain biological concepts shown in visuals
  • Represent biological models and diagrams
Science Practice 3

Questions and Methods

Designing and evaluating experimental procedures, including identifying variables and controls.

  • Identify independent and dependent variables
  • Identify and justify appropriate controls
  • Propose new investigations based on evidence
Science Practice 4

Representing and Describing Data

Constructing and interpreting graphs and data tables to communicate and identify patterns in biological data.

  • Choose the appropriate graph type for the data
  • Label axes with correct units and include a legend
  • Plot data accurately with error bars where appropriate
Science Practice 5

Statistical Tests and Data Analysis

Performing mathematical operations and statistical analysis to evaluate biological data and test hypotheses.

  • Perform calculations: means, rates, ratios, percentages
  • Use data to support or reject the null hypothesis
  • Apply chi-square and standard deviation where appropriate
Science Practice 6

Argumentation

Making and defending scientific claims using evidence and biological reasoning - the core skill for AP free-response questions.

  • Make a scientific claim
  • Support claims with evidence from biological principles and data
  • Predict the effects of changes or disruptions in biological systems
